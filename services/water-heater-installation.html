Types of Water Heaters

There are numerous types of water heaters on the market, including electric, solar, on-demand, conventional, storage tank, gas, tankless, and high-efficiency units.

Gas Water Heaters

Gas water heaters incur higher upfront costs than electric options, but they can save money long-term due to the low cost of natural gas. Periodically inspect your gas line, gas valve, and water tank to prevent leaks and sediment buildup.

Electric Water Heaters

Electric heaters usually offer lower upfront costs but generally cause higher monthly utility bills when compared to gas heaters. Because the electric grid powers them, they are more environmentally friendly — especially if local electricity uses renewable energy sources.

Natural Gas & Propane Heaters

Propane is derived from raw natural gas and heats water twice as fast while burning half as much gas per hour, making propane water heaters more energy-efficient than natural gas units. One downside is that propane tanks must be manually refueled.

Signs You Need a Water Heater Replacement

Rising energy bills, slow leaks, corroded tank liners, and a scarcity of hot water are all valid reasons to replace your water heater right away.

  • Water pooling or wet carpet: Likely an internal structural problem causing a slow leak from sediment buildup, a cracked liner, rust, a faulty drain valve, or a defective T&P relief valve.
  • High water bills: Often caused by a leak. A backed-up, old, or corroded heater set at an excessively high temperature can double costs.
  • Takes a long time for hot water: Tankless units or a hot water recirculation system can deliver hot water quickly without wasting gallons.
  • Gas unit constantly needs relighting: A defective thermocouple is the most likely cause of pilot light problems.

Should I Repair or Replace My Water Heater?

We recommend seriously considering a new water heater if:

  1. Your unit is providing insufficient hot water.
  2. Your unit is frequently breaking down and needing costly repairs.
  3. Your unit is over 10 years old, wearing out, and losing its efficiency.
  4. Your unit leaks or is rusting due to water minerals.

If the pilot light flickers out, the circuit breaker trips, the burner fails, the valve gets stuck, or the thermostat breaks, repair is usually inexpensive. New models are up to 20% more efficient — saving hundreds in energy costs over the unit's lifespan.

How Long Do Water Heaters Last?

A conventional storage tank water heater should function smoothly for 10 to 13 years. Electric tanks generally last 8 to 12 years. Tankless and solar water heaters typically last 20 to 30 years. Lifespan can be extended with proactive maintenance, a water softener, and regular sediment draining.

Benefits of Getting a New Water Heater

  • Delivering enough hot water to satisfy all your needs
  • Improving your energy efficiency
  • Reducing the likelihood of emergency breakdowns
  • Creating more space (especially with tankless)
  • Making less sound

New water heaters are energy-efficient and less costly than older units. Tankless systems, electric heat pumps, and solar water heaters offer even more significant savings. Some eco-friendly options may qualify for federal tax credits.

Why It's Important to Maintain Your Water Heater

A water heater can be a dangerous appliance if installed by an amateur and neglected. Maintenance safeguards your home from:

  • Water heater leaks that can cause fire hazards and mold
  • Gas line and flue leaks that can cause explosions and/or carbon monoxide poisoning
  • Faulty water temperature controls that can cause serious burns

Water Heater Replacement FAQs

What is the difference between gas and electric water heaters?

Gas water heaters use natural gas or propane; electric water heaters use electricity. Switching from electric to gas can be complicated, requiring extended gas lines and new vents. Electric heat pumps are usually more energy-efficient than conventional electric units.

Can I install a water heater myself?

We do not recommend DIY installation. An over-pressurized tank may explode, wiring mistakes increase electrocution and fire risks, and faulty gas line installation can cause carbon monoxide poisoning. Our experienced technicians can help you select the proper size and model and safely install it.

How long does it take a plumber to install a new water heater?

Our certified technicians can usually remove an old unit and install a new one in approximately three hours. Various factors can affect this timeframe, including the need to add new gas or water lines.

Are water heater replacements covered under homeowners insurance?

Some policies consider a water heater to be part of a home and cover it under dwelling coverage. Your water heater might also be insured by equipment breakdown coverage. Standard policies usually don't cover water heaters unless specifically added.

Do I need a permit for a new water heater?

Most cities and counties require permits to ensure a newly installed water heater meets current safety codes. Electrical modifications may require an electrical permit; gas or water line changes may require a plumbing permit.
